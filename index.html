01

Audience

Audience model

Most followers will never buy, and that's fine

The account needs a wide audience for reach and credibility. Inside that audience sits a smaller group with a real problem, and inside that group, the people who can pay for help.

Content audience

People who like fitness, physiques, training and food content. Many follow for entertainment or information.

High-relevance segments

A recurring problem: they quit, they lack time, an injury holds them back, or progress has stalled.

Core buyer

Clear problem, needs follow-up, can afford it.

Decision

Keep widening reach, and build a path inside the content that lets the core buyer feel Abdelrahman understands their exact situation.

02
Core buyer

Who is closest to the 12-week program

Someone for whom information alone has already failed. Man or woman, in Egypt or the Gulf.

What they look like

  • Has tried before, or is afraid of starting wrong.
  • Faces a constant obstacle: time, travel, eating out, an old injury.
  • Wants a plan built around their own life.
  • Can pay, and is ready to commit for 12 weeks.
In one sentence

Someone who has tried to fix their body before, or is scared to start wrong, and can't hold a routine or fit a plan into their life. They want a clear result, and above all a personal system and follow-up that keeps them going.

Why this person

Competitors win reach with workouts and physiques. The highest-value questions in their comments are about consistency, customization, pain, portions and time.

03
Persona 01 · Primary

The Restarter

#1
"I know how to start. I just can't keep going."

Has tried diets and gyms more than once. The first two weeks go perfectly. After one stressful week or a few messy days, back to zero.

What they want

  • A system they can actually live on.
  • Someone who pulls them back fast after a bad week.
  • A sense of moving in one direction.

What holds them back

  • "I tried before and didn't stick with it."
  • "I don't want to pay and fail again."
  • "I'll have to give up the food I love."

Content that works

  • What to do after a ruined day.
  • Client stories that include real setbacks.
  • Getting back on track after a slip.
EGYPTUsually slips because of money pressure or social events. The worry: "I'll pay and fall again."
GULFUsually slips because of frequent travel or a routine change. The worry: "Will this be any different from what I've tried?"
Message

You don't need a stronger start. You need a system that still works on the days that go off plan.

04
Persona 02 · Primary

The Busy Professional

#2
"I'm busy, and I don't want my whole life to become a diet."

Long workdays, shifting schedules, travel and restaurant meals. Wants results without strict meal prep or a rigid training split.

What they want

  • A flexible, clear plan.
  • The fewest daily decisions possible.
  • Options for travel and eating out.

What holds them back

  • "I don't have the time."
  • "My schedule keeps changing."
  • "I can't weigh every meal."

Content that works

  • Restaurant and travel eating.
  • 30 to 45 minute workouts.
  • "If your week falls apart, here's what we change."
EGYPTBusy means long hours, commuting and money pressure. Content should give tools for a packed day.
GULFBusy can mean the opposite: drivers, help at home, delivery food. Inactivity comes from comfort, so the message needs a reason to move.
Message

The right plan fits inside your life and keeps working when your week gets messy.

05
Persona 03 · High potential

The Careful Returner

#3
"I want to get back to training without hurting myself again."

Has had pain or an injury, such as a disc problem. Doesn't want to stop, but needs confidence in what to do and how.

What they want

  • Clear technique and progression.
  • Alternatives to the movements that hurt.
  • A gradual return instead of a full stop.

What holds them back

  • "How can an online coach know my condition?"
  • "Do I need treatment, not training?"
  • "Everyone gives me different advice."

Content that works

  • Mobility and warm-ups for specific problems.
  • Exercise swaps for disc issues.
  • When to stop and see a doctor.
EGYPTHesitation comes from the cost of physiotherapy, so they look for a cheaper way to train safely.
GULFHesitation comes from too many conflicting opinions. They need someone to settle the decision.
Message

Come back with a plan that knows what your body needs, when to add load and when to adjust.

06
Personas 04 to 06

Three more people in the audience

#4 · High potential

The Stuck Trainee

"I train, but I'm not changing."

Knows the gym. Needs to understand why results stopped: the program, food, recovery or execution.

What sells: showing the thinking behind a plan, and tracking with real numbers.

#5 · High potential

The Overwhelmed Beginner

"Everyone says something different. Where do I start?"

Decided to take care of their body, but keeps postponing under a pile of contradicting advice.

What sells: one clear first step, and someone who sets the priorities.

#6 · Emerging

The Medication Supporter

"I'm on a weight-loss drug. I don't want to lose my muscle."

Takes or considers a GLP-1 drug on medical advice, and wants to protect muscle, shape and health.

Red line: the natural route comes first. The drug is a decision for the doctor. Our role is support only.

Price reads differently by market

In Egypt the question is "can I afford it?". In the Gulf the same number is fair, and the question becomes "does cheap mean lower quality?".

Some buyers pick the best-known coach rather than the best fit. Results and method need to be visible, because nationality alone is not proof.

The open space

The angle nobody has taken

InForma is closest to Abdelrahman's niche, but leads with science and physique. The psychology of sticking to a plan is left open.

Illustrative placement from the content review.

Positioning

High trust and transparency, depth on the psychology of consistency, and time-efficient plans for busy people. All nine coaches are weak in these three areas.

1

Why you quit

Short stories about relapse, ending with one small fix.

2

Myth and its root

Break the myth, then name the emotional cause.

3

Honest transformations

Real journeys with plateaus, and a clear word on faked photos.

4

Behind the coaching

Answering real DM questions on camera.

14
Direction

What to borrow, and what to skip

Borrow

  • A clear idea in the first second: one hook, one problem, one result.
  • Practical content people want to save.
  • Real personality and presence.
  • A mix of teaching, stories, lifestyle and proof.

Skip

  • The same routine in the same template, with only the muscle changed.
  • Reach that has nothing to do with the service.
  • A CTA stuck on the end of the caption.
  • Physique content with no client results behind it.
The opportunity

A practical, human personal brand: content people save, a personality they remember, and a natural link to the service that brings the right leads.

Core message

A plan that fits you, and that you can keep following

Core message

The result you want doesn't need a rigid system. It needs a plan that fits your body, your life and your level, and one you can realistically keep following for 90 days.

1

A plan made for you

Built around your body, schedule, level, health and preferences.

2

A slip is a normal part of it

If you fall off in the middle of the plan, we adjust it and keep going.

3

We do it together

Regular follow-up, clear guidance and realistic adjustments.

What proves it

Client stories, check-ins and examples of a plan being changed when life changed.

17
Point of view

What he believes, and how it becomes messages

  • The best plan is the one you can keep following.
  • People usually need a simpler system, not more motivation.
  • Diet and training should adapt to the person.
  • A slip in the middle is a moment to review and adjust.
  • Follow-up and adjustment matter more than a fixed plan.
  • A fast result is worth little if it can't last.
  • The scale is one signal, not the only measure.
  • The psychology of sticking to it deserves as much attention as food and training.
